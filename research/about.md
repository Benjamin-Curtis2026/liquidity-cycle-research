I'm Benjamin Curtis, a biochemistry major at The University of Texas at Austin, class of 2028. I built this site to test, with data, the market ideas I follow most closely: how Federal Reserve liquidity moves risk assets, whether pullbacks to major weekly moving averages are worth buying, how markets react to the Fed, and which layers of the AI build-out (compute, optical networking, power, and software) are leading.

My training in the lab sciences shapes how I approach markets. Each study here states its question and its test before the results are known, measures every result against a baseline the way an experiment is measured against a control, and reports its limitations alongside its findings. The work draws on statistical inference (permutation tests, multiple-comparison corrections, and non-overlapping samples), event-study and backtesting methods with transaction costs and a cash yield, and macroeconomic data from the Fed's balance sheet and the Treasury market.

I'm pursuing a Master of Science in Finance to build the formal foundation in asset pricing, fixed income, and econometrics behind questions like these, with the goal of a career in financial analysis and advising, where explaining what the data says matters as much as finding it. Next on this project: adding global central-bank liquidity and the dollar to the liquidity measure, walk-forward testing of the regime rules, and valuation and capital-spending data for the AI infrastructure baskets.

**Data.** Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis (FRED) for balance-sheet, rate, and yield series; Yahoo Finance for adjusted daily prices; the Federal Reserve Board for FOMC dates.

**Tools.** Python (pandas, NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib), automated with GitHub Actions so the site rebuilds and republishes every week. I used AI-assisted coding tools in development; the research questions are mine.
